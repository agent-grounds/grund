# DF-number-name-sections: a name may follow a number (`1.goals`), and a cited address with one is locked

**Date:** 2026-10-13

## status: Accepted

## context: Context

With `[id] named_sections = true` a named component could occur at every depth of an all-name path and a number could follow a name (`goals.3`), but a name could not follow a number: `1.goals` was reserved ([§FS-config.3.3.1](../../functional-spec/FS-config.md#331-named-components)). The reservation came from the agora on [issue #178](https://github.com/agent-grounds/grund/issues/178#issuecomment-5509614230), whose ruling gave the reason: a `number.name` path's "drift is structurally uncatchable, because after a renumber the drifted parent still exists and, under uniform naming conventions, typically still owns a same-named child". Insert a new chapter `1` with its own `goals`, and a stored `<§>FS-x.1.goals` silently reads the new chapter's goals.

[Issue #662](https://github.com/agent-grounds/grund/issues/662) asks for a name under a number, because most specs are numbered and a stable name for one deep point otherwise means renaming every component above it ([§GOAL-friendliness-first](../../goals.md#goal-friendliness-first-as-user--and-agent-friendly-as-possible), [§REQ-spec-section-names.names](../../requirements/REQ-spec-section-names.md#names-a-name-says-what-the-thing-is)). Since #178 the section lock exists ([§FS-section-lock](../../functional-spec/FS-section-lock.md#fs-section-lock-a-cited-relative-section-keeps-its-text-or-check-says-where-the-text-went)): it records the text a cited address held and reports when that text turns up at another address. The author [directed the lift](https://github.com/agent-grounds/grund/issues/662#issuecomment-6142144475) "provided the lock covers every case the lift opens", and the agora on #662 established what that takes.

## decision: Decision

A name may follow a number at any depth: `1.goals`, `1.goals.latency` and `1.goals.3` are legal coordinates, written as a complete-path heading (`### 1.goals: Goals`) or, in a Markdown declaration body, as a relative one (`### .goals: Goals` under `## 1. Overview`). The two spellings are one address, read through the one resolver of relative headings, so writing both under one parent is a `duplicate-section` and there is still no dual numeric/name address ([§DF-relative-section-headings.amends](DF-relative-section-headings.md#amends-what-it-amends-in-the-named-sections-design)). A path in which a name comes after a number is a **mixed path** ([§FS-config.3.3.1](../../functional-spec/FS-config.md#331-named-components)).

### decision.lock: The lock is what answers #178

A cited address whose canonical path is mixed is enrolled in the section lock, whichever spelling declared it, as a relative heading's address already is ([§FS-section-lock.holds.entry](../../functional-spec/FS-section-lock.md#holdsentry-one-line-per-locked-address)). So the #178 scenario is caught: once `<§>FS-x.1.goals` is recorded, inserting a new chapter `1` with its own `goals` makes `check` report that what the lock recorded now sits at `FS-x.2.goals`, and a bare `grund fmt --write` leaves that report in place ([§FS-section-lock.write.never-a-move](../../functional-spec/FS-section-lock.md#writenever-a-move-fmt-never-approves-a-move)). It clears once the citation follows the move or the new `1.goals` holds a pointer to `FS-x.2.goals`. The #178 wording on #662, "fails `check` until `grund fmt --write` re-records it", would have pinned the opposite of the safeguard, so the pin asserts the refusal and then the two repairs.

One gap had to close first: a renumber rewrites every complete-path heading nested in the moved section, which made the move read as an edit in place. [§DF-section-lock-relative-fingerprint](DF-section-lock-relative-fingerprint.md#df-section-lock-relative-fingerprint-a-locked-sections-nested-headings-are-fingerprinted-relative-to-it-so-a-renumber-is-still-caught) fingerprints nested headings relative to the section, and that is the condition this lift rests on.

### decision.enrolment: What is enrolled

A resolved citation's address is enrolled exactly when its heading is relative or its canonical path has a numeric component followed, at any later depth, by a named one. The test reads the path's parsed components, never digits or letters in the ID. `FS-x.1.goals`, `FS-x.1.goals.3`, `FS-x.1.goals.latency` and `FS-x.goals.3.latency` are enrolled; complete-path `FS-x.goals.3`, `FS-x.1.2` and `FS-x.format.goals` are not, because enrolling them would turn passing citations of a legal construct into errors.

### decision.bounded: What the lock still does not catch

The guarantee is bounded, and the spec says where. The lock catches a leaf or nested-heading renumber of a scanned, enrolled citation whose entry was recorded before the move, whose text is unique in its declaration, and whose move changed nothing but heading paths. It does not catch a first citation written after the move, a deleted or dropped baseline ([§FS-section-lock.limits.baseline](../../functional-spec/FS-section-lock.md#limitsbaseline-a-baseline-the-lock-never-saw)), a move with an edit, including a self-citation the move updates ([§FS-section-lock.limits.edit-and-move](../../functional-spec/FS-section-lock.md#limitsedit-and-move-a-section-moved-and-edited-in-one-change)), identical texts ([§FS-section-lock.limits.identical](../../functional-spec/FS-section-lock.md#limitsidentical-identical-texts)), a move across declarations ([§FS-section-lock.limits.across](../../functional-spec/FS-section-lock.md#limitsacross-across-declarations)), or a citation the scan does not read. Each of those needs something besides the renumber, and each is exactly as unguarded for a `goals.3` citation today.

### decision.citations: A citation that was skipped is now checked

A marked citation of a mixed path was skipped without a word; now it is resolved. A citation of one that resolves to nothing, and a rule or `[citations]` finding that only such citations raise, are warnings in the release this lands in, naming the release in which they become errors ([§FS-citations.checks.mixed-path](../../functional-spec/FS-citations.md#checksmixed-path-a-citation-of-a-name-under-a-number-is-checked-and-warns-until-0190), [§FS-rules.7.12](../../functional-spec/FS-rules.md#712-a-finding-only-a-citation-of-a-name-under-a-number-raises-warns-until-0190), [§RM-mixed-path-citation-error](../../roadmap.md#rm-mixed-path-citation-error-make-a-citation-of-a-name-under-a-number-that-resolves-to-nothing-an-error-in-0190)), as [§REQ-backwards-compatibility.2](../../requirements/REQ-backwards-compatibility.md#2-the-deprecation-path) prescribes. The grammar change is a new licence rather than a correction, so <§>REQ-backwards-compatibility.5 is not used. A resolving citation with no lock entry is the immediate `unrecorded-section-citation` error, because one shipped command, `grund fmt --write`, repairs it ([§REQ-backwards-compatibility.3](../../requirements/REQ-backwards-compatibility.md#3-loud-mechanical-migrations)); for a mixed address its message names both releases ([§FS-section-lock.checks.unrecorded-section-citation](../../functional-spec/FS-section-lock.md#checksunrecorded-section-citation-a-locked-address-the-lock-does-not-hold)). In a tree that passes today it arises only through a stub whose target lies outside the scan. The flip is [#710](https://github.com/agent-grounds/grund/issues/710).

### decision.doc-comments: Doc-comments wait for #639

In a source doc-comment, both spellings stay prose in the release this lands in and warn `parse-moves`, naming the section they become and the release ([§FS-declarations.checks.parse-moves.2](../../functional-spec/FS-declarations.md#checksparse-moves2-the-five-shapes), shapes 4 and 5). For a mixed path the warning offers only a bold label or a code span to keep the line prose, never "write it complete", because the complete form is prose in that release too. Both gain their meaning together at the boundary where #639 gives doc-comment relative headings theirs ([§RM-doc-comment-relative-headings](../../roadmap.md#rm-doc-comment-relative-headings-give-a-relative-heading-in-a-doc-comment-its-meaning-in-0190)). A warning never ships in the release whose meaning it announces. Both took their meaning in 0.19.0 with #639, and the warning went with it ([§DF-one-parse-answers-from-0-19.release-note](DF-one-parse-answers-from-0-19.md#release-note-release-note)).

### decision.release: Releases

This lands in 0.18.0: the Markdown headings, the fingerprint, the lock, the citation warnings and the doc-comment warnings. The citation errors and the doc-comment meaning come in 0.19.0. If 0.18.0 is tagged before this merges, every date moves one release: the warnings ship in 0.19.0 and the errors and the meaning come no earlier than 0.20.0, and the doc-comment shapes take a code of their own, since `parse-moves` leaves in 0.19.0 ([§DF-doc-comment-relative-headings.decision.coupling](DF-doc-comment-relative-headings.md#decisioncoupling-coupled-to-639)).

### decision.values: `2.values` is an ordinary section

A value chapter must be a direct named chapter ([§FS-values.2.5](../../functional-spec/FS-values.md#25-chapter-declared-value-roots)), so `### 2.values:` is an ordinary named section with no value authority, and no binding verdict moves.

## rejected: Rejected alternatives

- **Keep the reservation.** The author ruled against it on #662: the lock now answers #178's reason.
- **A name/number alias,** `1.goals` as another address of section `1`. There is no dual addressing.
- **Enrol complete-path `goals.3`.** It would turn passing citations of a legal construct into errors.
- **Forbid complete-path headings under a mixed section.** It refuses the form the issue asks for; the fingerprint closes the gap instead.
- **Normalize citation text inside the fingerprint.** It would also hide a self-citation that genuinely changed its target ([§DF-section-lock-relative-fingerprint](DF-section-lock-relative-fingerprint.md#df-section-lock-relative-fingerprint-a-locked-sections-nested-headings-are-fingerprinted-relative-to-it-so-a-renumber-is-still-caught)).
- **Give doc-comment mixed headings their meaning at once.** It moves a verdict that passes today without a release of notice, and moves `refs`, ownership, citation directions and chapter counts one case at a time where deferral moves them once.

## release-note: Release note

- [§FS-config.3.3.1](../../functional-spec/FS-config.md#331-named-components), [§FS-declarations.section.relative.form](../../functional-spec/FS-declarations.md#sectionrelativeform-the-form), [§FS-section-lock.holds.entry](../../functional-spec/FS-section-lock.md#holdsentry-one-line-per-locked-address): **a name may follow a number in a section path (`1.goals`), and a cited one is locked.** With `[id] named_sections = true`, `### 1.goals: Goals` under `## 1. Overview`, or `### .goals: Goals` there, declares `<ID>.1.goals`, and `<ID>.1.goals.latency` may sit below it. A citation of such an address needs an entry in the section lock, like a relative heading's: run `grund fmt --write` after citing one and commit what it writes. Renumbering the chapter above it is then reported as a move rather than silently retargeting the citation. **Who this affects:** headings that were `unmarked-heading` errors become sections, which loosens a verdict; a run with `--ignore unmarked-heading` over a body holding them can now fail through `duplicate-section`, `orphan-section` or the lock codes. A marked citation of a mixed path, skipped until now, is resolved: one that resolves to nothing, and a rule or `[citations]` finding only such citations raise, warns in this release and becomes an error in 0.19.0; one whose heading sits in a stub's target outside the scan reports `unrecorded-section-citation` at once, which `grund fmt --write` fixes. `refs`, `cover` and `unused` count these citations. In a source doc-comment such a line stays prose and warns `parse-moves`; it becomes a section in 0.19.0. The managed agent block moves to v17, or v18 where a rule kind is enabled, so each repository carrying it reports `agents-init` once until `grund init` runs. Closes [issue #662](https://github.com/agent-grounds/grund/issues/662).
