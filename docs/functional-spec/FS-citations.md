# FS-citations: what a citation is, and where its text is never rewritten

What a citation is, which tokens a scan records as one, and which spans of text no writer rewrites, defined once for every command that reads or writes a citation: `check` judges these citations ([§FS-check](FS-check.md#fs-check-grund-validates-every-citation-in-a-repo)) and `fmt` rewrites them ([§FS-fmt](FS-fmt.md#fs-fmt-grund-normalizes-citations-in-bulk)). Serves [§GOAL-no-dangling-refs](../goals.md#goal-no-dangling-refs-every-cited-id-resolves-to-a-declaration), which a token recognized here is held to, and [§GOAL-polyglot-citation](../goals.md#goal-polyglot-citation-ids-cite-cleanly-from-anywhere-they-are-useful), which is why a citation is recognized in prose, comments and doc-comments alike.

## terms: Terms

Leans on [§FS-terms.terms.2](FS-terms.md#terms2-citations) (marker, citation, qualified citation, shorthand, canonical form,
citation site), [§FS-terms.terms.3](FS-terms.md#terms3-source-forms) (doc-comment), and [§FS-terms.terms.4](FS-terms.md#terms4-scanning-and-project-structure) (scan, scope, config root).
The suppressed scope is [§FS-fmt.terms](FS-fmt.md#terms-terms)'s.

- **recognized citation** — A token the scan records as a citation edge. It is what `refs` lists,
  what counts as a use and grounds a unit, and what every check judges.
- **never-rewrite zone** — A span whose bytes every pass preserves exactly, because a rewrite there
  would change what the file means. Which spans they are is [§FS-citations.zones](FS-citations.md#zones-where-citation-text-is-never-rewritten).
- **wrap** — A citation written as a Markdown link whose text is the citation: the form
  `grund fmt --cross-refs` writes and keeps current.
- **enclosing link** — A Markdown inline link its author wrote whose text holds a citation and
  more besides, `[the §<ID> record](…)`: the author's, never a wrap. Which links enclose is
  [§FS-citations.wrap.enclosing](FS-citations.md#wrapenclosing-a-citation-inside-a-link-its-author-wrote-is-left-to-the-author).

## requirements: What every reading of a citation holds to

Every reading here holds to [§REQ-no-missed-citation](../requirements/REQ-no-missed-citation.md#req-no-missed-citation-every-citation-the-run-reads-is-checked) and its bounded blind spots ([§REQ-no-missed-citation.2](../requirements/REQ-no-missed-citation.md#2-every-blind-spot-is-declared-and-bounded)), to [§REQ-one-answer.contract](../requirements/REQ-one-answer.md#contract-one-question-one-tree-one-answer), and to [§FS-check.requirements.no-declined-fix](FS-check.md#requirementsno-declined-fix-an-error-never-names-a-fix-grund-declines-to-make--realized-directional-for-the-next-finding), which [§FS-citations.rewrite-exemption](FS-citations.md#rewrite-exemption-where-the-text-forbids-the-rewrite) serves.

### requirements.whole-token: A token cites what it spells whole, or nothing

Each recognition rule reads its whole candidate before deciding what it is, so no token is recorded as a citation of a prefix of what its author wrote, whether the rest is a shorthand's tail, a letter-bearing or mixed section tail, a glob operator, a partial declaration-local tail or an off-format spelling ([§FS-citations.shorthand.full-id-wins](FS-citations.md#shorthandfull-id-wins-the-full-id-always-wins), [§FS-citations.recognized.named-candidates](FS-citations.md#recognizednamed-candidates-named-section-candidates), [§FS-citations.recognized.globs](FS-citations.md#recognizedglobs-a-glob-operator-never-shortens-a-marked-citation), [§FS-citations.recognized.local-sections](FS-citations.md#recognizedlocal-sections-declaration-local-numeric-section-candidates), [§FS-citations.recognized.off-format](FS-citations.md#recognizedoff-format-a-marked-token-its-kinds-format-does-not-spell-is-reported-never-dropped)). What is not a citation whole, a pattern, unsupported syntax, an off-format citation or prose, earns no edge to, and no finding about, an ID or section its author did not write. One case is deferred: a canonical citation whose section component runs on into word characters, `<§>FS-042-user-login.1x`, is recorded as a citation of section `1`, with named sections on or off, while the shorthand `<§>FS-042.1x` is no citation at all, so the two forms disagree about the one boundary [§FS-citations.shorthand.full-id-wins](FS-citations.md#shorthandfull-id-wins-the-full-id-always-wins) says they share. Serves [§REQ-no-wrong-citation.1](../requirements/REQ-no-wrong-citation.md#1-no-wrong-resolution) and [§REQ-no-wrong-citation.2](../requirements/REQ-no-wrong-citation.md#2-no-false-alarms).

### requirements.edge-or-finding: A context removes a token's edge or withholds one finding, and says which

Every context that changes how a token is read does one of two things, to every consumer at once. An **exclusion** removes the edge: the token is no citation for `refs`, `cover`, unused-declaration counting, grounding, citation directions or chapter rules ([§FS-citations.recognized.contexts](FS-citations.md#recognizedcontexts-contexts-read-as-neither-prose-nor-code), [§FS-citations.recognized.link-destinations](FS-citations.md#recognizedlink-destinations-markdown-link-destinations), [§FS-citations.recognized.escapes](FS-citations.md#recognizedescapes-an-id-in-an-escape-position), [§FS-citations.recognized.globs](FS-citations.md#recognizedglobs-a-glob-operator-never-shortens-a-marked-citation)). An **exemption** keeps the edge whole and withholds one finding, the mechanical demand of the shorthand error ([§FS-citations.rewrite-exemption](FS-citations.md#rewrite-exemption-where-the-text-forbids-the-rewrite)). A token the scan does record, off-grammar, behind a word-character marker or a shorthand, is one edge every consumer reads ([§FS-citations.recognized.off-grammar](FS-citations.md#recognizedoff-grammar-off-grammar-citations), [§FS-citations.recognized.marker-start](FS-citations.md#recognizedmarker-start-the-configured-marker-establishes-the-citation-start), [§FS-citations.shorthand.edge](FS-citations.md#shorthandedge-a-resolved-shorthand-is-a-real-edge)). A context added from here on says which of the two it is and reaches every consumer accordingly; one that would keep an edge from some consumers and not others is neither, and needs its own decision. Serves [§REQ-no-missed-citation.2](../requirements/REQ-no-missed-citation.md#2-every-blind-spot-is-declared-and-bounded), whose blind spots are bounded only because each is total, and [§REQ-no-wrong-citation.2](../requirements/REQ-no-wrong-citation.md#2-no-false-alarms).

## recognized: Recognized citations

A citation is the marker followed by an ID, as in `§FS-check.3.1` ([§DF-reference-marker](../decisions/functional/DF-reference-marker.md#df-reference-marker-use--as-the-reference-marker-with--as-the-typing-trigger)). The marker is `§` unless `[reference] marker` names another; under the default `strict = true` only a marked citation is recognized, and `strict = false` recognizes a bare full ID as well ([§FS-config.3.1](FS-config.md#31-reference--citation-form)). A citation is recognized in Markdown prose and in a source file's comments and doc-comments ([§FS-languages.comment-block](FS-languages.md#comment-block-the-comment-block)), outside the contexts of [§FS-citations.recognized.contexts](FS-citations.md#recognizedcontexts-contexts-read-as-neither-prose-nor-code). An `E2E` citation resolves against the case directories of the `E2E` kind home ([§FS-declarations.e2e-case.id](FS-declarations.md#e2e-caseid-a-case-directory-and-the-id-it-declares)).

### recognized.off-grammar: Off-grammar citations

An off-grammar citation that a catalog declaration backs ([§FS-config.3.2](FS-config.md#32-id--id-grammar)) participates in section and dangling checks, inbound counts, grounding, citation directions, `refs`, formatting, and editor navigation exactly like a conforming citation. The declaration's mismatch is reported at its heading ([§FS-declarations.checks.declaration-near-miss](FS-declarations.md#checksdeclaration-near-miss-declaration-near-miss)), not at every citation.

### recognized.named-candidates: Named-section candidates

When `[id] named_sections = true`, the scanner consumes a whole ID-and-dot-tail candidate before deciding what it means ([§FS-config.3.2](FS-config.md#32-id--id-grammar)). A marked legal named or mixed coordinate ([§FS-config.3.3.1](FS-config.md#331-named-components)) is a citation even when its section is missing, resolved whole and never truncated to its numeric prefix ([§FS-citations.checks.mixed-path](FS-citations.md#checksmixed-path-a-citation-of-a-name-under-a-number-is-checked)). An unmarked candidate with a letter-bearing tail is one prose token, suppressed whole even under `strict = false`, and never falls back to a bare-ID citation. Inside its own declaration the local form `<§>2.goals` stays one unsupported token ([§FS-citations.recognized.local-sections](FS-citations.md#recognizedlocal-sections-declaration-local-numeric-section-candidates)).

### recognized.string-literals: String literals in source files

In a source file, a **bare** ID-shaped token whose start column falls inside a string literal is no citation, by the quote tracking `grund fmt` uses ([§FS-fmt.2.3.1](FS-fmt.md#231-string-literal-exclusion-rule)), so an ID-shaped substring inside runtime data does not raise a false dangling citation. A marked unqualified citation is recognized in every string, the marker being the signal of intent, except in assigned Python data ([§FS-citations.recognized.string-literals.assigned-data](FS-citations.md#recognizedstring-literalsassigned-data-assigned-python-triple-quoted-data)); a qualified one is skipped ([§FS-citations.recognized.contexts](FS-citations.md#recognizedcontexts-contexts-read-as-neither-prose-nor-code)). Both rules hold on every line a multi-line literal spans ([§FS-languages.multi-line-string](FS-languages.md#multi-line-string-a-string-literal-that-spans-lines)).

#### recognized.string-literals.assigned-data: Assigned Python triple-quoted data

In a file whose docstrings are read ([§FS-fmt.2.3.1](FS-fmt.md#231-string-literal-exclusion-rule)), a simple unindented assignment whose right-hand side begins with a triple-quoted string opens an **assigned-data span**, not a docstring. Its left-hand side is one Python identifier with an optional same-line annotation, and after `=` comes the string, `'''` or `"""`, carrying, case-insensitively, no prefix or one of `r`, `u`, `b`, `f`, `t`, `br`/`rb`, `fr`/`rf` or `tr`/`rt`. A parenthesized, destructuring, computed or indented assignment, and a string reached only after another expression, open none: the reading is lexical, not Python scope or AST analysis ([§FS-non-goals.3](FS-non-goals.md#3-code-ast-parsing)).

The span runs from the prefix, or the opening delimiter, to the next matching delimiter that no odd run of backslashes escapes, on its line or a later one. Nothing inside it is recognized: no citation form, declaration, section heading, value tag or binding, or note. Text outside it keeps ordinary source treatment, so a docstring after it opens as it would without the assignment ([§DF-python-assigned-triple-quoted-data](../decisions/functional/DF-python-assigned-triple-quoted-data.md#df-python-assigned-triple-quoted-data-module-level-assigned-triple-quoted-strings-are-data-not-docstrings)).

### recognized.link-destinations: Markdown link destinations

In a Markdown file, the parallel carve-out is the link destination: a **bare** ID-shaped token whose start column falls inside an inline link's `(…)` half is no citation for any consumer, because no pass rewrites a link destination ([§FS-citations.zones.link-destination](FS-citations.md#zoneslink-destination-a-link-destination-is-the-url)) and a finding whose only fix the tool refuses is one a repository can never clear ([§FS-check.3.13](FS-check.md#313-number-only-shorthand-citation)). A marked citation there is recognized. In a destination's other three spellings a bare token is a citation as anywhere, because skipping it would move verdicts that nothing licenses ([§REQ-backwards-compatibility.5](../requirements/REQ-backwards-compatibility.md#5-correcting-a-verdict-another-requirement-forbids)); only its bytes are protected ([§FS-citations.zones.link-destination](FS-citations.md#zoneslink-destination-a-link-destination-is-the-url)), and `check` withholds the mechanical demand there ([§FS-citations.rewrite-exemption](FS-citations.md#rewrite-exemption-where-the-text-forbids-the-rewrite)).

### recognized.contexts: Contexts read as neither prose nor code

Two contexts are read as neither prose nor code, so nothing inside them is a citation. A **fenced code block** in Markdown is skipped entirely: this is what makes an example ID safe to write in documentation without the `<§>` escape. What opens and closes a fence is [§FS-languages.fence](FS-languages.md#fence-fences); a fence whose tag `[schema.sources] fenced` selects ([§FS-languages.fence.language](FS-languages.md#fencelanguage-a-fences-tag-and-the-fences-fenced-selects)) is the exception, its citations ordinary edges. In **source files only**, the qualified form `§<alias>/<ID>` is skipped inside an inline-code span or a string literal, a multi-line one included, because `alias/ID` is shaped like a path, module reference or URL; the unqualified form stays live there. An assigned Python data span is skipped too ([§FS-citations.recognized.string-literals.assigned-data](FS-citations.md#recognizedstring-literalsassigned-data-assigned-python-triple-quoted-data)); every other skip is a file the scan does not read ([§REQ-no-missed-citation.2](../requirements/REQ-no-missed-citation.md#2-every-blind-spot-is-declared-and-bounded), [§FS-check.1.3](FS-check.md#13-the-full-tree-scope---full)).

A CommonMark raw-text HTML block ([§FS-check.1.1.5.1](FS-check.md#1151-a-raw-html-block-holds-no-heading)) and a Markdown file's front matter ([§FS-check.1.1.5.2](FS-check.md#1152-front-matter-holds-no-heading)) are read as raw text for structure but as prose for citations. Citations inside the block stay live, and so do those in front matter: each resolves, counts as a use and is reported when it dangles, and one in front matter belongs to the file's first declaration ([§FS-check.1.1.5.2.1](FS-check.md#11521-its-citations-belong-to-the-files-first-declaration)).

A prose apostrophe in a supported source comment, and a Rust lifetime or a Haskell identifier prime in the code before a trailing comment, open no string literal, so a qualified citation in that comment is recognized and judged as it is without them, `unknown project alias` included ([§DF-qualified-comment-quotes](../decisions/functional/DF-qualified-comment-quotes.md#df-qualified-comment-quotes-recognize-qualified-comment-citations-after-nonliteral-single-quotes)). `fmt`'s rewrites and a bare token keep the raw quote toggle of [§FS-fmt.2.3.1](FS-fmt.md#231-string-literal-exclusion-rule).

### recognized.value-bindings: Value bindings

An exact explicit value binding also records its authored component, but its marked token remains one citation under every rule above. The binding grammar and the narrower source-comment contexts it is recognized in are [§FS-values.3](FS-values.md#3-explicit-value-bindings); `[reference] strict = false` never removes the binding's marker requirement.

### recognized.snapshots: Fetched snapshots

A snapshot written by [§FS-fetch](FS-fetch.md#fs-fetch-grund-materializes-one-external-fact-snapshot) is not a special input. Its configured file or folder home is in the ordinary scan scope ([§FS-config.3.5](FS-config.md#35-scan--what-gets-scanned)), and its declaration, body, sections and marked body citations are recognized by the rules above. `check` never invokes its configured integration.

### recognized.local-sections: Declaration-local numeric section candidates

A configured marker followed immediately by decimal components separated by `.`, whatever separator the config sets between an ID and its section, is a declaration-local section candidate, under both strict modes; an unmarked number stays prose. Full IDs and the shorthand claim their tokens first, and escapes and the contexts of [§FS-citations.recognized.contexts](FS-citations.md#recognizedcontexts-contexts-read-as-neither-prose-nor-code) keep their precedence.

The candidate is a whole token: a tail that would leave its numeric prefix partial, as in `<§>2.goals`, `<§>2abc` or `<§>2..1`, keeps the whole token for the unsupported-syntax verdict of [§FS-check.3.24](FS-check.md#324-declaration-local-section-citation) and records no edge. One dot after a complete token is sentence punctuation; a run of them is unsupported syntax. Named and mixed local forms are not recognized.

A candidate is owned only by the declaration whose body encloses it ([§FS-declarations.body](FS-declarations.md#body-where-a-declarations-body-ends)), never by an adjacent citation or another file. A uniquely owned candidate is an ordinary edge to its owner's ID and numeric path, keeping its local token text; an ownerless or ambiguous one records no target ([§DF-declaration-local-section-shorthand](../decisions/functional/DF-declaration-local-section-shorthand.md#df-declaration-local-section-shorthand-local-numeric-section-citations-are-recognized-but-never-canonical)).

### recognized.escapes: An ID in an escape position

A **bare** ID-shaped token that begins immediately after the literal escape, the configured marker wrapped in `<` and `>`, is no citation for any consumer, in Markdown and source files alike and under both strict modes, because the marker is not immediately followed by the ID ([§FS-check.2.3.1](FS-check.md#231-escaped-citation-resolves)) and no pass rewrites an escape position ([§FS-citations.zones](FS-citations.md#zones-where-citation-text-is-never-rewritten)). The qualified `<§>alias/ID` is covered too, not only through [§FS-workspace.1.3](FS-workspace.md#13-the-shape-outside-a-workspace). The escape is spelled with the configured marker: where it is `@`, `<@>ID` is the escape and a `<§>`-wrapped token is an ordinary bare one. In both modes the `escaped-citation-resolves` suggestion ([§FS-check.2.3.1](FS-check.md#231-escaped-citation-resolves)) is the only report of such a site ([§DF-escape-position-is-not-a-citation](../decisions/functional/DF-escape-position-is-not-a-citation.md#df-escape-position-is-not-a-citation-an-escape-position-is-not-a-citation-in-either-strict-mode)).

### recognized.marker-start: The configured marker establishes the citation start

The configured marker immediately followed by a full ID establishes a citation start even when the marker ends in a word character, such as `_`: with `[reference] marker = "_"` and `[id] format = "{kind}_{slug}"`, `_FS_login` is exactly one marked citation of `FS_login` under either strict mode, located at the marker's column, and a missing target is an ordinary dangling finding. Only the explicit marker starts a token this way: under `strict = false` an unmarked ID still needs its ordinary token boundary. Escapes and every context exclusion apply first ([§DF-word-character-citation-markers](../decisions/functional/DF-word-character-citation-markers.md#df-word-character-citation-markers-recognize-accepted-word-character-markers)).

### recognized.globs: A glob operator never shortens a marked citation

A citation names one exact point, so a glob operator, `*`, `?`, a `[` class or a `{` alternation, never ends a marked token early to leave a citation of its prefix. The marked **candidate** runs from the marker over every address character, section separator and operator that follows; a `[` is an operator only when an address character or `!` follows it, a `{` only when an address character, `,` or the section separator follows it, `[^…]` is a footnote reference, never a class, and a brace group may cross the separator ([§FS-coordinate-globs.grammar.braces](FS-coordinate-globs.md#grammarbraces-brace-groups), [§DF-coordinate-globs.4](../decisions/functional/DF-coordinate-globs.md#4-the-citation-guards-verdict-correction)). It is read in this order:

1. **Catalog first.** A candidate that is a declared ID, with or without a section path, is that citation whatever characters it holds ([§FS-declarations.line.configured-slug](FS-declarations.md#lineconfigured-slug-characters-admitted-by-the-slug-pattern-belong-to-the-canonical-id)).
2. **Grammatical but undeclared.** A candidate the effective address grammar ([§FS-config.3.2](FS-config.md#32-id--id-grammar)) accepts whole is a citation with the ordinary verdicts ([§FS-check.3.1](FS-check.md#31-dangling-citation), [§FS-check.3.2](FS-check.md#32-missing-section)).
3. **A pattern.** Any other candidate holding an operator is consumed whole and is a pattern: no edge to its prefix or to anything else, no `fmt` rewrite, and no `escaped-citation-resolves` suggestion for its escaped form ([§FS-check.2.3.1](FS-check.md#231-escaped-citation-resolves)). It is reported as [§FS-check.checks.glob-citation](FS-check.md#checksglob-citation-glob-citation).

A `*` or `?` run that directly follows a complete ID or section path, and is followed by no address character, operator, or section separator leading to one, is punctuation: `**<§>FS-login**.`, `<§>FS-login?` and `<§>FS-login[^1]` cite the ID before them. Under `strict = false` an unmarked candidate holding an operator is one prose token, suppressed whole like a letter-bearing tail ([§FS-citations.recognized.named-candidates](FS-citations.md#recognizednamed-candidates-named-section-candidates)). Escapes and the contexts of [§FS-citations.recognized.contexts](FS-citations.md#recognizedcontexts-contexts-read-as-neither-prose-nor-code) apply first ([§DF-glob-citation](../decisions/functional/DF-glob-citation.md#df-glob-citation-a-glob-operator-ends-no-citation-and-the-prefix-it-left-behind-is-removed)).

### recognized.off-format: A marked token its kind's format does not spell is reported, never dropped

A marked token that opens like an ID of a configured kind is a citation or an **off-format citation**, never text. Where no exact declaration backs it ([§FS-config.3.2.5](FS-config.md#325-off-grammar-declarations-stay-readable)) it records no edge, `grund fmt` rewrites nothing in it, and `show` and `refs` refuse it as an invalid ID ([§FS-show.3.5](FS-show.md#35-the-hint-for-each-failure)). It is reported once, as [§FS-check.checks.off-format-citation](FS-check.md#checksoff-format-citation-off-format-citation), when three things hold:

1. **No other reading claims its marker.** A full-ID citation, resolved or dangling ([§FS-check.3.1](FS-check.md#31-dangling-citation)), a catalog-backed off-grammar citation ([§FS-citations.recognized.off-grammar](FS-citations.md#recognizedoff-grammar-off-grammar-citations)), a shorthand ([§FS-citations.shorthand](FS-citations.md#shorthand-the-number-only-shorthand)), a pattern ([§FS-citations.recognized.globs](FS-citations.md#recognizedglobs-a-glob-operator-never-shortens-a-marked-citation)) and a qualified token whose alias is unknown ([§FS-check.3.8](FS-check.md#38-cross-project-citation-failure)) each keep their own verdict and earn no second.
2. **It opens with a configured kind.** Its ID begins with exactly one configured citable kind, then a character that is not an ASCII letter or digit, the test [§FS-declarations.checks.declaration-near-miss.1](FS-declarations.md#checksdeclaration-near-miss1-what-counts) applies to a heading, and then at least one ASCII letter or digit. So `<§>XYZ-1`, where nothing configures `XYZ`, and a prefix mention such as `<§>REQ-` or `<§>REQ-<slug>` stay text.
3. **Its kind's format does not spell it**, whole or followed by a section path ([§FS-config.3.2](FS-config.md#32-id--id-grammar)). A heading's citation of its own in-format ID is not reported; [§FS-citations.recognized.title-lines](FS-citations.md#recognizedtitle-lines-a-declarations-heading-cites-what-it-names) drops it.

An unqualified token is judged by its own project's grammar and catalog, a qualified one by those of the project its alias names. The **token** is the ID part, a qualified token's alias and `/` included, read over ASCII letters, digits, `-`, `_` and `.`, so a section path is part of it and the token is named whole; one `.` that ends the run is sentence punctuation. It is reported exactly where a marked citation written the same way is recognized and silent exactly where such a citation is, so it adds no skip to [§REQ-no-missed-citation.2](../requirements/REQ-no-missed-citation.md#2-every-blind-spot-is-declared-and-bounded) and removes none ([§DF-off-format-citation](../decisions/functional/DF-off-format-citation.md#df-off-format-citation-a-marked-token-its-kinds-format-does-not-spell-and-nothing-declares-is-reported-rather-than-dropped)).

### recognized.title-lines: A declaration's heading cites what it names

A declaration's heading line is read for citations like any other line, except that a citation of the ID that heading declares is no citation, because a declaration does not use itself. Every other citation on it, the `<§>FS-b` in `# <§>FS-a: Alpha, built on <§>FS-b`, is an ordinary edge from that declaration, listed at the heading's line. A section heading is read the same way, and so is the heading of a declaration [§FS-declarations.checks.declaration-near-miss](FS-declarations.md#checksdeclaration-near-miss-declaration-near-miss) flags, which declares all the same ([§FS-config.3.2.5](FS-config.md#325-off-grammar-declarations-stay-readable)): its declared ID is its token as written, and only a citation of that token is dropped ([§DF-a-near-miss-title-citation-is-a-citation](../decisions/functional/DF-a-near-miss-title-citation-is-a-citation.md#df-a-near-miss-title-citation-is-a-citation-a-citation-on-a-near-miss-declarations-heading-is-a-citation-as-on-any-declarations)). `grund fmt` never rewrites a declaration's heading line ([§FS-fmt.2.3](FS-fmt.md#23-what-is-never-rewritten)), and a title citation is no index entry ([§DF-a-title-citation-is-a-citation](../decisions/functional/DF-a-title-citation-is-a-citation.md#df-a-title-citation-is-a-citation-a-citation-on-a-declarations-heading-is-a-citation-unless-it-cites-the-id-that-heading-declares)).

### recognized.no-kinds: With no kind, no token is a citation

An ID opens with a configured kind ([§FS-terms.terms.1](FS-terms.md#terms1-declarations-and-coordinates)), so a project whose config declares no kinds ([§FS-config.3.4.4.5](FS-config.md#3445-kinds---declares-no-kinds)) recognizes no citation at all: `check` reports none of its marked tokens, dangling or otherwise, and its run ends on the caution that the project declares no kinds ([§FS-check.4.5.2](FS-check.md#452-the-message)), at exit `0`, because a project that has declared no kind yet is an unfinished setup rather than a broken one.

## shorthand: The number-only shorthand

When a kind's effective format carries **both** `{number}` and `{slug}` ([§FS-config.3.2](FS-config.md#32-id--id-grammar)), as the default `{kind}-{number}-{slug}` that `grund init` writes does, the number alone already identifies a declaration within its kind, so `<§>FS-042` is an abbreviation of `<§>FS-042-user-login` rather than a different ID. `check` **recognizes** and resolves that shape whatever the project's `[reference] shorthand` policy, which decides only whether a resolved shorthand may persist ([§FS-check.3.13](FS-check.md#313-number-only-shorthand-citation)), so a shorthand is never silently ignored ([§GOAL-no-dangling-refs](../goals.md#goal-no-dangling-refs-every-cited-id-resolves-to-a-declaration), [§DF-number-only-citation-shorthand](../decisions/functional/DF-number-only-citation-shorthand.md#df-number-only-citation-shorthand-the-number-only-shorthand-is-authoring-sugar-and-a-persisted-one-is-a-check-error)).

### shorthand.shape: The shorthand shape

The shorthand shape is the kind's effective format with the `{slug}` placeholder and one adjacent literal separator removed. A kind whose effective format has no `{number}` (`{kind}-{slug}`, the form `grund` itself uses) or no `{slug}` (`{kind}-{number}`) has no shorthand ([§FS-id.4.1](FS-id.md#41-number-less-id-formats)).

### shorthand.marker: The marker is required

A shorthand carries no slug to make an accidental match unlikely, so the marker is what supplies the intent: a bare `FS-042` is plain text even under `strict = false`, where a bare full ID would count ([§FS-citations.recognized](FS-citations.md#recognized-recognized-citations), [§DF-number-only-citation-shorthand.2.4](../decisions/functional/DF-number-only-citation-shorthand.md#24-the-marker-is-required-a-bare-shorthand-is-text)).

### shorthand.full-id-wins: The full ID always wins

The full-ID pass claims its tokens first, and the shorthand pass only sees what is left. It claims a token only where the token ends: the character after the match is no alphanumeric, no `_`, and no literal from `format` that has a component after it. A separator in the text counts only when a component follows it, so a citation may end a sentence. A full ID whose slug the grammar rejects, `<§>FS-042-User-Login` or `<§>FS-042_user_login`, is therefore no shorthand with a tail but no citation at all, reported whole where nothing declares it ([§FS-citations.recognized.off-format](FS-citations.md#recognizedoff-format-a-marked-token-its-kinds-format-does-not-spell-is-reported-never-dropped)). A `/` never continues an ID, because it can only precede a kind: `<§>FS-042/x` cites `FS-042` as `<§>FS-042-user-login/x` cites the full ID, so the shorthand and the canonical form share one boundary.

### shorthand.edge: A resolved shorthand is a real edge

When a shorthand matches exactly one declaration, the citation participates in the graph like any other: [§FS-refs](FS-refs.md#fs-refs-grund-lists-every-citation-of-an-id) lists it, [§FS-cover](FS-cover.md#fs-cover-grund-groups-citations-by-scanned-file) groups it, it keeps its declaration from being reported unused ([§FS-check.4.1](FS-check.md#41-unused-declaration)), it grounds its file under `require_grounding` ([§FS-check.3.6](FS-check.md#36-ungrounded-unit-opt-in)), and it counts for citation directions ([§FS-config.3.9](FS-config.md#39-citations--citation-direction-rules)). Its `.<section>` suffix is checked as on any citation ([§FS-check.3.2](FS-check.md#32-missing-section)): a shorthand carrying a section that does not exist earns *both* findings, because the canonical form [§FS-check.3.13](FS-check.md#313-number-only-shorthand-citation) names is the right ID and still the wrong section.

### shorthand.numeric-run: A shorthand in a numeric run

A recognized shorthand glued to a second number, `§SPEC-001→SPEC-003`, is a numeral in a run: it resolves and counts like any other edge, but `grund fmt` does not rewrite it ([§FS-fmt.2.4.1](FS-fmt.md#241-a-shorthand-in-a-numeric-run-is-not-rewritten)), and [§FS-check.3.15](FS-check.md#315-shorthand-citation-in-a-numeric-run) reports it in place of [§FS-check.3.13](FS-check.md#313-number-only-shorthand-citation).

### shorthand.cli: The shorthand as a CLI ID argument

The same shape is accepted as a **CLI ID argument**, `grund FS-042`, `grund FS-042.1`, `grund refs FS-042`, where nothing is persisted and the caller gets the declaration ([§FS-show.1](FS-show.md#1-inputs), [§FS-refs.1](FS-refs.md#1-inputs)). That is what makes a clicked `<§>FS-042` open in a terminal or editor, since those clients hand the token straight to `grund` ([§FS-integrations.3.1](FS-integrations.md#31-terminal-clients-wezterm-kitty-tmux-iterm2)).

## zones: Where citation text is never rewritten

Some spans hold text that is legitimate exactly as it stands, so no pass of `grund fmt` rewrites a citation there and each keeps its bytes. These zones are forced by the text itself, never asked for by the repository:

- string literals on a source line, where a rewrite would change runtime behavior ([§FS-fmt.2.3.1](FS-fmt.md#231-string-literal-exclusion-rule));
- a Markdown **fenced code block**, in every pass ([§FS-fmt.2.3.3](FS-fmt.md#233-a-fenced-code-block-is-an-illustration));
- Markdown inline code spans (where rewriting would change a literal command, path, or example), ending where [§FS-citations.zones.inline-code](FS-citations.md#zonesinline-code-an-inline-code-span-closes-on-a-run-of-its-own-length) says;
- Markdown link destinations, in each of their four spellings, except a destination `fmt` itself maintains ([§FS-citations.zones.link-destination](FS-citations.md#zoneslink-destination-a-link-destination-is-the-url));
- a Markdown file's **front matter**, in every pass ([§FS-check.1.1.5.2](FS-check.md#1152-front-matter-holds-no-heading), [§DF-front-matter](../decisions/functional/DF-front-matter.md#df-front-matter-a-markdown-files-front-matter-is-a-region-of-its-own-owned-by-the-files-first-declaration-and-never-rewritten));
- a reference key, and a raw HTML tag outside an `href` value ([§FS-citations.zones.authored-markup](FS-citations.md#zonesauthored-markup-a-reference-key-and-an-html-tag-keep-their-bytes));
- an escape position ([§FS-citations.recognized.escapes](FS-citations.md#recognizedescapes-an-id-in-an-escape-position)), where `--marker` would turn an illustration into a live citation ([§DF-escape-position-is-not-a-citation](../decisions/functional/DF-escape-position-is-not-a-citation.md#df-escape-position-is-not-a-citation-an-escape-position-is-not-a-citation-in-either-strict-mode)); every later pass, the cross-reference wrap included, follows the marker and stops with it.

Where `check` withholds the demand for a rewrite is [§FS-citations.rewrite-exemption](FS-citations.md#rewrite-exemption-where-the-text-forbids-the-rewrite), which names its own zones.

### zones.link-destination: A link destination is the URL

ID-shaped text inside a Markdown link destination is not rewritten, because rewriting it would change the URL rather than the visible citation. Which bare tokens in a destination are citations is [§FS-citations.recognized.link-destinations](FS-citations.md#recognizedlink-destinations-markdown-link-destinations). A destination is spelled four ways: the `(…)` of an inline link or an image, with the extent below; a reference definition's destination, `[label]: <destination> "title"`, to the end of its line, title included, because every reference that uses the definition takes the title along; an autolink, `<scheme:…>` or `<user@host>`, its `<` and `>` kept; and the value of a raw HTML tag's `href` attribute, quoted or unquoted, quotes kept. No pass rewrites a byte of any of them, the cross-reference wrap no more than a trigger, `--marker` or a shorthand expansion. The one exception is the destination of a cross-reference wrap `fmt` itself maintains, which every cross-reference pass re-derives ([§FS-fmt.6.3](FS-fmt.md#63-idempotency-and-re-derive)): a **closed** wrap's, whose brackets pass the test of [§FS-citations.wrap.recognized](FS-citations.md#wraprecognized-an-existing-wrap-is-recognized-by-its-brackets). Every other destination is the author's ([§DF-fmt-never-writes-into-a-link-destination](../decisions/functional/DF-fmt-never-writes-into-a-link-destination.md#df-fmt-never-writes-into-a-link-destination-fmt-never-writes-into-a-link-destination-it-does-not-maintain)), a hand-authored one and every byte after a `](` that never closes alike.

An inline destination runs from the `(` after an unescaped `](` to the `)` that balances it, each unescaped parenthesis inside opening or closing one level and an escaped one counting for neither, or to the end of the line where none balances it. Every pass of `fmt` and every reading `check` makes of the zone take this one extent.

### zones.authored-markup: A reference key and an HTML tag keep their bytes

Two spans are the author's markup rather than prose, and no pass of `grund fmt`, nor the LSP on-type transform ([§FS-lsp.1.4](FS-lsp.md#14-live-trigger-transform)), rewrites a byte in them:

- **A reference key**: the `[key]` of a full reference `[text][key]`; the text of a collapsed reference `[text][]`, and of a shortcut reference `[text]` that a definition matches, each its own key; and a definition's label, `[label]: …`. A reference's key and its definition's label are one key spelled twice, so rewriting either would unlink the reference ([§DF-fmt-preserves-authored-markup](../decisions/functional/DF-fmt-preserves-authored-markup.md#df-fmt-preserves-authored-markup-fmt-leaves-a-citation-in-every-link-form-its-author-wrote-and-in-an-image-and-check-credits-only-the-ones-that-go-somewhere)). The visible text of a full reference is no key, and is rewritten as an inline link's text is ([§FS-citations.wrap.enclosing](FS-citations.md#wrapenclosing-a-citation-inside-a-link-its-author-wrote-is-left-to-the-author)).
- **A raw HTML tag**, from its `<` to the first `>` outside a quoted attribute value on its line, outside an `href` value, which is a link destination ([§FS-citations.zones.link-destination](FS-citations.md#zoneslink-destination-a-link-destination-is-the-url)). An element's content is text a reader sees, not a tag.

A citation in either span is recognized, resolved and checked as anywhere else ([§REQ-no-missed-citation.2](../requirements/REQ-no-missed-citation.md#2-every-blind-spot-is-declared-and-bounded)); only the mechanical demand is withheld ([§FS-citations.rewrite-exemption](FS-citations.md#rewrite-exemption-where-the-text-forbids-the-rewrite)). Which brackets are a key is [§FS-citations.wrap.enclosing.shape](FS-citations.md#wrapenclosingshape-which-links-enclose).

### zones.inline-code: An inline code span closes on a run of its own length

Where a Markdown inline code span begins and ends is read one line at a time, the way CommonMark reads it:

- it opens at a run of *n* backticks whose first backtick is not backslash-escaped;
- it closes at the next run of **exactly** *n* backticks, so a shorter or a longer run inside it is content;
- inside the span a backslash is literal and does not stop the closing run, so `` `C:\` `` is a closed span;
- an opener with no closing run on its line leaves the rest of that line code.

The last rule departs from CommonMark, which reads such an opener as literal backticks, because reading code costs at most a rewrite not made ([§REQ-no-wrong-citation.3](../requirements/REQ-no-wrong-citation.md#3-no-wrong-write), [§DF-inline-code-span-closes-on-its-own-run](../decisions/functional/DF-inline-code-span-closes-on-its-own-run.md#df-inline-code-span-closes-on-its-own-run-an-inline-code-span-closes-on-a-run-of-its-own-length)).

One reading serves every caller, because they have to agree: every pass of `grund fmt`, the LSP on-type transform ([§FS-lsp.1.4](FS-lsp.md#14-live-trigger-transform)), and every reading `check` makes of inline code, which are the canonical-form exemption ([§FS-citations.rewrite-exemption](FS-citations.md#rewrite-exemption-where-the-text-forbids-the-rewrite)), the illustration hint on a dangling citation ([§FS-check.3.1.2](FS-check.md#312-an-illustration-in-inline-code)), the kind-index entry form ([§FS-check.3.17.4](FS-check.md#3174-only-a-citation-fmt-would-wrap-reaches-this-rule)) and the source-file skip of a qualified citation ([§FS-citations.recognized.contexts](FS-citations.md#recognizedcontexts-contexts-read-as-neither-prose-nor-code)).

## rewrite-exemption: Where the text forbids the rewrite

A shorthand inside inline code ([§FS-citations.zones.inline-code](FS-citations.md#zonesinline-code-an-inline-code-span-closes-on-a-run-of-its-own-length)), a Markdown link destination in any of its spellings ([§FS-citations.zones.link-destination](FS-citations.md#zoneslink-destination-a-link-destination-is-the-url)), a reference key or an HTML tag ([§FS-citations.zones.authored-markup](FS-citations.md#zonesauthored-markup-a-reference-key-and-an-html-tag-keep-their-bytes)), or a source string literal is exempt from the resolving form of the shorthand error ([§FS-check.3.13](FS-check.md#313-number-only-shorthand-citation)), because [§FS-fmt.2.3](FS-fmt.md#23-what-is-never-rewritten) forbids the rewrite in those four contexts, where the text is legitimate as it stands. These four are the whole list: a zone added to what `fmt` never rewrites does not join it by being added there. A suppressed scope and an external file-symlink target are not among them: `fmt` does not rewrite there either, but the error fires and the author clears it by hand ([§FS-fmt.2.5](FS-fmt.md#25-suppressed-scopes), [§FS-fmt.2.3.2](FS-fmt.md#232-a-link-that-leaves-the-config-root-is-not-written-through)). Nor is a fence `[schema.sources] fenced` selects, where under `canonical` a shorthand draws the resolving form and the author writes the full ID ([§FS-languages.fence.language](FS-languages.md#fencelanguage-a-fences-tag-and-the-fences-fenced-selects), [§DF-fenced-citations](../decisions/functional/DF-fenced-citations.md#df-fenced-citations-a-markdown-fence-is-read-for-citations-only-when-schemasources-fenced-names-its-tag)). The exemption is for the *mechanical* form only: the exempt citation resolves and counts ([§FS-citations.shorthand.edge](FS-citations.md#shorthandedge-a-resolved-shorthand-is-a-real-edge)), and a shorthand matching zero or several declarations is still reported there, because that is a dangling citation rather than a formatting nit.

## wrap: The wrap

A wrap is the form `grund fmt --cross-refs` writes ([§FS-fmt.6.2](FS-fmt.md#62-form)). Every citation in a Markdown file is read one of three ways: a **wrap** ([§FS-citations.wrap.recognized](FS-citations.md#wraprecognized-an-existing-wrap-is-recognized-by-its-brackets)), a citation inside an **enclosing link** ([§FS-citations.wrap.enclosing](FS-citations.md#wrapenclosing-a-citation-inside-a-link-its-author-wrote-is-left-to-the-author)), or **unwrapped**. The reading is of the whole document, because a reference finds its definition anywhere in the file and a link's text may run onto later lines. One reading serves every caller ([§REQ-one-answer.contract](../requirements/REQ-one-answer.md#contract-one-question-one-tree-one-answer)): `fmt`'s link pass, `check`'s kind-index entry form and its external enrollment, `show`'s flatten and the editor's hover linkification ([§FS-lsp.1.2.1](FS-lsp.md#121-citation-preview)).

### wrap.shape: The shape, never the target

`check` requires the **shape**, the citation wrapped as `[§<ID>…](<target>)`, and never the target, which every `grund fmt --cross-refs` pass re-derives ([§FS-fmt.6.3](FS-fmt.md#63-idempotency-and-re-derive), [§DF-index-entry-form](../decisions/functional/DF-index-entry-form.md#df-index-entry-form-an-index-entry-is-one-full-link-per-id-and-nothing-else-about-the-page)). Two shapes are a kind-index entry: the wrap, and a citation inside an enclosing link whose destination is not empty, such as `- [§<ID>: Use Postgres for all data](use-postgres.md)` ([§FS-citations.wrap.enclosing](FS-citations.md#wrapenclosing-a-citation-inside-a-link-its-author-wrote-is-left-to-the-author)). Neither has its target checked. Only the wrap is `fmt`'s to write and re-derive, and only the canonical wrap enrolls an external source declaration ([§FS-check.3.18.3](FS-check.md#3183-an-external-source-declaration-enrolls-by-its-canonical-link)).

### wrap.recognized: An existing wrap is recognized by its brackets

A citation is already wrapped when its immediately-preceding character is `[` and its immediately-following text is `](…)` closed by the `)` that balances its `(`, and that `[` does not follow an unescaped `!`, which opens an image's description ([§FS-citations.wrap.enclosing.images](FS-citations.md#wrapenclosingimages-an-image-is-not-a-link)). The one test decides both the re-derive of [§FS-fmt.6.3](FS-fmt.md#63-idempotency-and-re-derive) and the no-double-wrap rule, and `fmt`, `show`'s flatten ([§FS-show.3.2](FS-show.md#32-cross-reference-links-are-flattened-in-text-and-json)) and `check`'s kind-index entry form ([§FS-check.3.17.4](FS-check.md#3174-only-a-citation-fmt-would-wrap-reaches-this-rule)) read it alike. A citation that fails it is wrapped fresh unless an enclosing link holds it ([§FS-citations.wrap.enclosing](FS-citations.md#wrapenclosing-a-citation-inside-a-link-its-author-wrote-is-left-to-the-author)) or it is an image's whole description ([§FS-citations.wrap.enclosing.images](FS-citations.md#wrapenclosingimages-an-image-is-not-a-link)). So a wrap's whole destination is re-derived, `[§<ID>](old(1).md)` keeping no `.md)`, and a `[§<ID>](` whose destination never closes is no wrap, even with a later `)` on its line, and is wrapped fresh with the text after it kept ([§DF-fmt-and-editor-answers-from-0-19](../decisions/functional/DF-fmt-and-editor-answers-from-0-19.md#df-fmt-and-editor-answers-from-0-19-fmts-link-wrapper-and-the-editors-title-span-change-in-0190-after-a-release-that-announces-them)).

The test reads only the citation's own brackets. In a line whose wrap stands inside outer brackets, `- [[§<ID>](use-postgres.md#…): Use Postgres for all data](use-postgres.md)`, the inner `[§<ID>](…)` is the link, as CommonMark reads it, a wrap `fmt` keeps re-deriving and `check` takes as the entry, and the outer brackets are text, not an enclosing link. Such a line is a fixed point under `fmt --write` and `fmt --check`, and `show` collapses only the inner wrap.

### wrap.enclosing: A citation inside a link its author wrote is left to the author

A citation stands inside an **enclosing link** when it is in the text of a Markdown link its author wrote, in any form [§FS-citations.wrap.enclosing.shape](FS-citations.md#wrapenclosingshape-which-links-enclose) reads, and is not itself a wrap: `[the §<ID> record](adr/use-postgres.md)` or `See [the §<ID> rationale][pg].` An inline link whose text is the citation alone is the wrap ([§FS-citations.wrap.recognized](FS-citations.md#wraprecognized-an-existing-wrap-is-recognized-by-its-brackets)); every other form encloses whatever its text holds, so `[§<ID>][pg]` encloses too. Its author wrote that link, so it is theirs and not a wrap `fmt` keeps:

- **`fmt` leaves the link as written.** The link pass neither wraps the citation nor re-derives the destination, in every run that reaches it, the run that wraps a kind's index under `enabled = false` or in a suppressed scope included ([§FS-fmt.6.4](FS-fmt.md#64-what-is-never-wrapped)). Only the link is protected, never the citation in it: outside a reference key and an HTML tag ([§FS-citations.zones.authored-markup](FS-citations.md#zonesauthored-markup-a-reference-key-and-an-html-tag-keep-their-bytes)), a trigger, `--marker` and a shorthand expansion rewrite its token as anywhere ([§FS-fmt.2](FS-fmt.md#2-behavior)), and the citation is recognized, resolved and checked like any other ([§REQ-no-missed-citation](../requirements/REQ-no-missed-citation.md#req-no-missed-citation-every-citation-the-run-reads-is-checked)).
- **`check` takes it as a kind-index entry where the link goes somewhere** ([§FS-citations.wrap.shape](FS-citations.md#wrapshape-the-shape-never-the-target)): an inline link whose destination is not empty, and a full, collapsed or shortcut reference whose key a definition with a destination that is not empty matches. It never compares that destination with the one `fmt` would derive, and each citation in the text counts for its own ID, so `[§<A> and §<B>](both.md)` is an entry for both. No other enclosing form is an entry, a definition's label, an autolink and a raw HTML `<a>` among them ([§DF-index-entry-form.2.1](../decisions/functional/DF-index-entry-form.md#21-the-entry-is-a-recognized-citation-of-the-id-written-as-a-full-markdown-link)), and no enclosing form enrolls an external source declaration ([§FS-check.3.18.3](FS-check.md#3183-an-external-source-declaration-enrolls-by-its-canonical-link)); an ID with no entry falls to [§FS-check.3.18](FS-check.md#318-declaration-missing-from-its-kinds-index).
- **`show` prints it as written**, because only a wrap collapses ([§FS-show.3.2](FS-show.md#32-cross-reference-links-are-flattened-in-text-and-json)).

Decided in [§DF-fmt-preserves-authored-link-labels](../decisions/functional/DF-fmt-preserves-authored-link-labels.md#df-fmt-preserves-authored-link-labels-fmt-leaves-a-citation-in-a-link-its-author-wrote-and-check-takes-that-link-as-the-index-entry) and [§DF-fmt-preserves-authored-markup](../decisions/functional/DF-fmt-preserves-authored-markup.md#df-fmt-preserves-authored-markup-fmt-leaves-a-citation-in-every-link-form-its-author-wrote-and-in-an-image-and-check-credits-only-the-ones-that-go-somewhere).

#### wrap.enclosing.shape: Which links enclose

A citation is enclosed in every form a Markdown link is written in:

- an inline link, `[text](destination)`;
- a full reference, `[text][key]`, or a collapsed one, `[text][]`, whether or not a definition matches it;
- a shortcut reference, `[text]`, only where a definition matches its text;
- a definition line's label, `[label]: destination`;
- an autolink, `<scheme:…>` or `<user@host>`, whose whole is a destination ([§FS-citations.zones.link-destination](FS-citations.md#zoneslink-destination-a-link-destination-is-the-url));
- the content of a raw HTML `<a>` element, its tag names matched without regard to case, from its opening tag, which ends where [§FS-citations.zones.authored-markup](FS-citations.md#zonesauthored-markup-a-reference-key-and-an-html-tag-keep-their-bytes) says, to the `</a>` that closes it or to its paragraph's end; an HTML comment opens no element.

The forms are recognized by the extents below, which every other reading of the file uses, not by a Markdown parser, so malformed syntax neither names encloses nothing. A bracketed form is read across the lines of its paragraph. A paragraph ends at a blank line and at a line that opens a fenced code block, an ATX heading, a list item, empty or numbered from any number, or a thematic break, each read behind the line's `>` markers; a line with more `>` markers than the paragraph began with opens a quote, and a paragraph, of its own. Every other line continues the paragraph, an HTML block's opening line and a line with fewer markers, or none, included.

The text opens at a `[` that is neither backslash-escaped, inside an inline code span ([§FS-citations.zones.inline-code](FS-citations.md#zonesinline-code-an-inline-code-span-closes-on-a-run-of-its-own-length)), nor an image's ([§FS-citations.wrap.enclosing.images](FS-citations.md#wrapenclosingimages-an-image-is-not-a-link)), and closes at the `]` that balances it, an escaped or code-span bracket counting for neither, so `[the [draft] §<ID> record](…)` encloses. A `[` that no `]` balances before its paragraph ends opens no link, unless it opens an incomplete label (below). What follows the `]` decides the form:

- a `(` makes an inline link, whose destination has the one extent of [§FS-citations.zones.link-destination](FS-citations.md#zoneslink-destination-a-link-destination-is-the-url), and which is no link when that destination never closes on its line ([§FS-citations.wrap.recognized](FS-citations.md#wraprecognized-an-existing-wrap-is-recognized-by-its-brackets));
- a `[` makes a full or collapsed reference where its key closes on that line;
- a `:` makes a definition only where the `[` opens its line behind at most three spaces or behind the line's block-quote and list markers, the line starts its paragraph or follows only definitions in it, and the rest of the line is a destination, `<…>` or a run with no whitespace whose parentheses balance, and at most a title after it, set off by whitespace, in `"…"`, `'…'` or `(…)` and closed on that line;
- a `[^` opens a footnote, not a label;
- anything else is a shortcut reference where a definition matches the text, and plain brackets where none does.

A key is matched case-folded, each whitespace run read as one space and the ends trimmed. A definition may stand anywhere in the file outside a fenced code block, the first of two for one key wins, and nothing outside the file is read ([§REQ-runs-offline.1](../requirements/REQ-runs-offline.md#1-read-and-verification-paths-execute-nothing)).

Brackets in the text balance like any others, a link's included, so `[a [b](c.md) §<ID> d](x.md)` encloses the citation and, in a kind's index, is the entry for it, although CommonMark renders `[b](c.md)` as the link: taking the credit away would turn a passing `check` into a failing one that no prohibition stands behind ([§REQ-backwards-compatibility.5](../requirements/REQ-backwards-compatibility.md#5-correcting-a-verdict-another-requirement-forbids)).

An **incomplete label** is a `[` that no `]` balances before its paragraph ends because a backtick run after it has no closing run on its line, as in ``[the §<ID> `record](use-tick.md)``, so the rest of the line is code ([§FS-citations.zones.inline-code](FS-citations.md#zonesinline-code-an-inline-code-span-closes-on-a-run-of-its-own-length)). The label runs to its paragraph's end, the link pass leaves every citation in it as written, and none of them is an entry ([§FS-check.3.18](FS-check.md#318-declaration-missing-from-its-kinds-index)), though CommonMark would read the link whole: writing that guess into the file is the wrong write the inline-code zone refuses.

#### wrap.enclosing.images: An image is not a link

An image, `![description](file)`, is neither a link nor a wrap: a `[` after an unescaped `!` opens a description, which never encloses and fails the wrap test ([§FS-citations.wrap.recognized](FS-citations.md#wraprecognized-an-existing-wrap-is-recognized-by-its-brackets)), and its destination is the image's file, of which no pass rewrites a byte. A citation in a description is read one of two ways:

- **A citation that is an image's whole description**, `![§<ID>](cache.png)`, is left as written by the link pass, while the earlier passes rewrite its token as in a link's text. It is no link to the declaration, so it is no kind-index entry and enrolls no external source declaration ([§FS-citations.checks.image-index-entry](FS-citations.md#checksimage-index-entry-a-citation-only-image-is-no-index-entry-a-spent-check)).
- **A citation in a description that holds more**, `![a diagram of §<ID>](diagram.png)`, is wrapped by `fmt` as any unwrapped citation is, giving `![a diagram of [§<ID>](…)](diagram.png)`, and `check` reads it as bare, never as an entry. An index row already wrapped that way keeps its credit through the inner wrap, which is a wrap by its own brackets ([§FS-citations.wrap.recognized](FS-citations.md#wraprecognized-an-existing-wrap-is-recognized-by-its-brackets)).

Decided in [§DF-fmt-preserves-authored-markup](../decisions/functional/DF-fmt-preserves-authored-markup.md#df-fmt-preserves-authored-markup-fmt-leaves-a-citation-in-every-link-form-its-author-wrote-and-in-an-image-and-check-credits-only-the-ones-that-go-somewhere).

## citing-kind: Which kind a citation site counts as

Every citation site has exactly one **citing kind**, the kind a `[citations.<kind>]` rule names it by ([§FS-config.3.9](FS-config.md#39-citations--citation-direction-rules)), decided in this order:

1. **The enclosing declaration's kind.** The nearest preceding declaration whose body contains the site ([§FS-declarations.body](FS-declarations.md#body-where-a-declarations-body-ends)) gives its kind; a site in front matter is enclosed by the file's first declaration ([§FS-check.1.1.5.2.1](FS-check.md#11521-its-citations-belong-to-the-files-first-declaration)). A site later in the file than that body does not inherit it and falls through.
2. **The kind of the file's home.** Where no declaration encloses the site, the kind whose `folder` or `file` contains the site's file gives its kind, but only when exactly one configured home contains it.
3. **The homeless kind.** Otherwise, outside every home or in a file two overlapping homes contain, the site is the homeless kind's ([§FS-config.3.9.2](FS-config.md#392-the-homeless-kind)).

So the files of a non-citable kind count as that kind by where they are ([§FS-config.3.4.1.1](FS-config.md#3411-what-a-non-citable-kind-keeps)). An `E2E` case's obligations are judged per case, over its `spec.refs` lines and any citation sites the run scans inside it, and a run over the project scans none there ([§FS-declarations.e2e-case.walk](FS-declarations.md#e2e-casewalk-the-walk-stops-at-a-case-directory), [§FS-config.3.9.1.2](FS-config.md#3912-e2e-obligations-are-per-case)).

## checks: Checks

Each section is named for a check, and none reports a finding under that name.

### checks.image-index-entry: A citation-only image is no index entry (a spent check)

This check never fires, because a citation-only image is no index entry and enrolls nothing ([§FS-citations.wrap.enclosing.images](FS-citations.md#wrapenclosingimages-an-image-is-not-a-link), [§DF-image-index-entry-lands-in-0-20](../decisions/functional/DF-image-index-entry-lands-in-0-20.md#df-image-index-entry-lands-in-0-20-a-citation-only-image-is-no-index-entry-from-0200)): an ID whose index holds only such an image is `missing-index-entry` ([§FS-check.3.18](FS-check.md#318-declaration-missing-from-its-kinds-index)). The repair is `[§<ID>](<link>)` written beside the image, which may stay, where `<link>` is the link `grund fmt --cross-refs` writes from the index to the declaration ([§FS-check.3.18.5.2](FS-check.md#31852-what-not-listed-says)).

The code is in the catalog ([§FS-errors.5.5](FS-errors.md#55-the-check-code-catalog)): `--only image-index-entry` and `--ignore image-index-entry` are accepted and select nothing ([§FS-check.1.4](FS-check.md#14-selecting-findings-with---only-and---ignore)), so `--ignore` leaves the `missing-index-entry` error visible, and `--only` selects no replacement error, keeps the selected report's exit, and prints the off line of [§FS-check.1.4.1](FS-check.md#141-a-selected-check-the-run-leaves-off-says-so), which names the replacement to select:

```text
warning: --only image-index-entry judged nothing — the check is spent from grund 0.20.0; select missing-index-entry instead
```

### checks.unclosed-wrapper: Unclosed wrapper (a removed check)

No check reports `unclosed-wrapper` and no catalog row names it, so `--only unclosed-wrapper` and `--ignore unclosed-wrapper` are refused as unknown codes ([§FS-check.1.4](FS-check.md#14-selecting-findings-with---only-and---ignore)). A wrap is closed only by the `)` that balances its `(` ([§FS-citations.wrap.recognized](FS-citations.md#wraprecognized-an-existing-wrap-is-recognized-by-its-brackets), [§DF-fmt-and-editor-answers-from-0-19](../decisions/functional/DF-fmt-and-editor-answers-from-0-19.md#df-fmt-and-editor-answers-from-0-19-fmts-link-wrapper-and-the-editors-title-span-change-in-0190-after-a-release-that-announces-them)).

### checks.mixed-path: A citation of a name under a number is checked

With named sections enabled, a marked mixed path ([§FS-config.3.3.1](FS-config.md#331-named-components)) is resolved whole like any other citation ([§FS-citations.recognized.named-candidates](FS-citations.md#recognizednamed-candidates-named-section-candidates)). If its declaration exists but its section does not, it is an ordinary `missing-section` error ([§FS-check.3.2](FS-check.md#32-missing-section)); if its ID is undeclared, an ordinary `dangling` error ([§FS-check.3.1](FS-check.md#31-dangling-citation)). Both carry the ordinary messages, codes and sites ([§DF-mixed-path-citation-errors.decision](../decisions/functional/DF-mixed-path-citation-errors.md#decision-decision)). No check reports `mixed-path` and no catalog row names it, so `--only mixed-path` and `--ignore mixed-path` are refused as unknown codes ([§FS-check.1.4](FS-check.md#14-selecting-findings-with---only-and---ignore)). A resolving mixed citation counts for `refs`, `cover`, `unused` and the editor, and a missing lock entry is [§FS-section-lock.checks.unrecorded-section-citation](FS-section-lock.md#checksunrecorded-section-citation-a-locked-address-the-lock-does-not-hold). Rule and configured citation verdicts on it are [§FS-rules.7.12](FS-rules.md#712-a-finding-only-a-citation-of-a-name-under-a-number-raises-warns-until-0190)'s.
